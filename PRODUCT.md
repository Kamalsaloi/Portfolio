# Product context

## What this is

Kamal Saloi's design portfolio. A single-purpose surface: convince a founder or
a hiring manager, inside about ninety seconds, that this is a designer who can
think about the product, design the experience, and ship the frontend himself.

**register: brand**

The design IS the product here. Every page is a piece of evidence, not a
container for evidence.

## Users

A founder, a head of product, or a design hiring manager. They are skimming.
They have four other tabs open. They are not reading paragraphs until something
earns it. They are looking for one thing: does this person make good decisions,
and can they prove it.

They are not other designers looking for inspiration. Dribbble polish without a
decision behind it reads as empty to this audience.

## Voice

Three words: **plainspoken, exact, unhurried.**

- Plainspoken: say the thing. "A modal is for a decision you make and dismiss.
  A proposal is a document you read."
- Exact: real numbers, real screens, real constraints. Never "improved the
  experience." Always what changed and what it cost.
- Unhurried: the writing does not oversell. Confidence reads as calm.

Write the way the work was actually decided, first person, past tense, with the
reasoning intact. The reader should feel like they are being let in on the
thinking rather than pitched.

## Anti-references

Things this portfolio must not look or sound like:

- **The AI case study template.** Mono uppercase eyebrow, then a heading, then
  three paragraphs, on an identically tinted section, repeated six times. Every
  section the same colour is the tell.
- **Process theatre.** Double diamonds, persona cards, "empathise / define /
  ideate", stock sticky-note photos. Nobody hiring believes these.
- **Metric inflation.** "Increased engagement by 340%" with no baseline and no
  method.
- **Dribbble shots.** Perspective mockups on gradient backgrounds with no
  argument attached.
- **Restated headings.** A paragraph that says what the heading just said.

## Strategic principles

1. **One decision per section.** If a section does not contain a decision and
   its cost, cut it.
2. **Evidence beats adjectives.** Show the screen, name the constraint, give the
   number. Do not describe how good it is.
3. **State the change once.** The old state and the new state get said exactly
   one time each, never restated in a caption and a blurb and a body paragraph.
4. **Honesty is a feature.** Say what is unfinished, unmeasured, or uncertain.
   It reads as judgment, not weakness, and it is the same principle the Vantage
   product itself is built on.
5. **Sections earn their own art direction.** The narrative decides the visual
   world, not a template applied uniformly down the page.

## Current work

**Vantage** (AiDOOS): an AI that reads a company from the outside (its website,
job posts and reviews, then its tools once connected), finds one process that
keeps going wrong, and suggests a loop that fixes it. A loop checks its own
result against a goal and keeps adjusting until it gets there. Approved loops
move into AiDOOS, which builds and runs them.

It started as an opportunity engine that suggested what to build next. Cold
calls with distributors and a past client showed the real demand was fixing one
specific broken process, so it now puts those loops first.

This is the lead case study. It is the strongest UI work and the only one with
user research and competitive analysis behind it.

**Aidoos proposal flow**: the second case study. An AI-generated proposal flow,
from a homepage chat input through intake to a priced proposal.
